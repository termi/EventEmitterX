## Human-Readable Summary

Forgotten signals can be collected despite registration in global channels on runtimes with native WeakRef. Finalization
eventually detaches their resources. Live signals retain their callbacks. Explicit disposal cleans resources even when
user cleanup throws and reports aggregated errors. Already-aborted owners and failed construction are cleaned, and
closed/destroyed subscriptions cannot resume.

## Technical Details

EventSignal owns callback records and registers weak wrappers for dependencies, subscribers, external sources, triggers
and abort. Cleanup metadata excludes the owner and covers outgoing relationships as well as incoming registrations.
React component/RAF callbacks follow the same ownership rule. Global emitter instances are preserved for this first
step; instance channels remain the next separately committed change.

AI reasoning: separate leak repair from architectural relocation, preserve callback identity and notification semantics,
and verify forgotten versus live owners independently. A standalone expose-gc script covers nine collection scenarios
and a live control; package.json adds `test:signals:gc`. Six deterministic lifecycle regressions and the corrected audit
probe complement it. Roadmap EN/RU documents record scope and limitations.

## Reasons

Code-review refinements replace non-null assertions with local callback snapshots, name callback map/record types and
subscription flags, move reusable listener-record creation to `weakSignalCallback.ts`, clarify remaining destructor
cleanup entries, and broaden registration rollback without changing notification behavior.

[Weak notification ownership](reasons/EventSignal_WEAK_NOTIFICATION_OWNERSHIP.md) records the retaining path,
lexical-scope requirement, finalization metadata invariants, alternatives, examples and fallback guarantees.

## Original User Request
